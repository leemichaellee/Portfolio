Retirement Beneficiary Designation

Digitizing a process that had always lived on paper — letting clients designate and manage retirement account beneficiaries directly in their digital experience, with no existing pattern to build from and a legal/compliance domain I had to learn from scratch.

Role
Sr. Product Designer — inherited as lead designer mid-project, directing 1 content strategist, delivered to dev
Status
Launched, live (Nov 2024)
Scale
$5M project
Timeframe
Q4 2023 – Q4 2024 (launched Nov 2024)

Problem framing

Before this project, designating a beneficiary on a retirement account was a paper process — clients filled out and submitted a physical form, disconnected entirely from their digital account experience. My job was to digitize it: design a net-new experience with no existing digital pattern in the product to build from.

The domain itself was the first real challenge. Beneficiary designation carries a lot of legal and compliance weight, and it was a topic I was largely unfamiliar with going in — data validation rules around beneficiary names and share percentages, how to structure the information architecture for something this legally sensitive, and what "correct" even looks like from a compliance standpoint all had to be learned as part of doing the work.

Constraints

Unfamiliar legal and compliance domain

Beneficiary designations come with real legal weight, and the rules governing them (validation of beneficiary information, how allocations must work) weren't things the design team already had deep expertise in — a meaningful part of the work was learning the domain well enough to design it correctly.

No existing pattern to build from

Because this had only ever existed on paper, there was no prior digital IA or interaction pattern to adapt — the structure had to be designed from first principles rather than evolved from something that already existed.

Data validation complexity

Beneficiary share percentages across multiple people must sum to exactly 100% for a designation to be valid — a constraint that had to be designed for clearly, so clients could self-correct rather than submit an invalid designation.

Decisions

Decision 1 — A dedicated beneficiary designations homepage

The question
With no existing pattern to build from, what should the entry point into beneficiary management actually contain, given clients often have multiple accounts and may be doing this for the first time?
Options considered
A minimal, single-flow entry point directly into the designation form, vs. a dedicated homepage that orients clients before they start and gives them visibility across all their accounts.
The call
Built a dedicated homepage with a "What you'll need" section to prepare clients before they start, plus a card giving an overview of all the client's accounts and their current beneficiaries, with the ability to manage or update from there.
Why
Beneficiary designation is unfamiliar and legally weighty for most clients — orienting them first, and giving them an at-a-glance view across every account rather than forcing an account-by-account flow, matches how people actually think about this task.
How I led it
I defined this structure and content as I took over as lead designer on the project.

Decision 2 — Enforcing exact 100% allocation

The question
How do you guide clients to submit a valid, legally correct allocation across multiple beneficiaries without confusion or error?
Options considered
Freeform percentage entry with validation only at submission, vs. designing the flow to make the 100%-total requirement clear and self-correctable as the client enters allocations.
The call
Designed the allocation step to enforce that share percentages across all named beneficiaries sum to exactly 100% before a client can proceed.
Why
This is a hard legal requirement, not a soft validation — catching it clearly during entry prevents clients from submitting a designation that would later be rejected or create downstream compliance issues.
How I led it
I designed the validation pattern for this step.

Decision 3 — Applying designations across accounts

The question
Clients frequently want the same beneficiaries across multiple accounts. Should they have to re-enter the same information for every account?
Options considered
Require full re-entry per account (the simpler build) vs. letting clients apply an existing account's beneficiary settings to a new account.
The call
Built the ability for clients to apply beneficiary designation settings from an existing account directly to a new one.
Why
This was one of the biggest streamlining opportunities in the whole project — it removes repetitive, error-prone re-entry for what's likely the most common real-world case.
How I led it
I identified and pushed for this as a priority feature rather than a nice-to-have.

Reflection

Same lesson as the other work on this site: I'd have pushed for broader UX strategy thinking earlier, and a deeper understanding of beneficiary designation's legal implications and constraints, before getting into the detail of individual screens.

Across this project and the other two here, there's a consistent throughline in how I'm growing as a designer — moving from strength in execution toward strength in direction: understanding a domain and a product deeply enough to set the strategy, not just design the screens that follow from it.
