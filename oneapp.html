Problem framing

Five separate mobile experiences, each with its own information architecture, navigation model, and visual language, needed to function as one coherent product. Users with relationships spanning multiple Bank of America product lines were stuck juggling multiple apps, multiple logins, and multiple mental models for a single banking relationship.

The design problem wasn't a visual refresh — it was resolving genuine structural conflicts between five different ideas of "how the app is organized" without simply picking one existing structure and forcing the others to conform, which tends to break the experience for whichever app didn't win.

Add what the five original experiences were (names/purposes) and a concrete example of where their IA genuinely conflicted — e.g. two apps both had a similar concept structured in incompatible ways.

Constraints

Conflicting IA and navigation models

Each source app had deeply embedded navigation patterns and user habits built around its existing structure. Reconciling them meant more than picking a winner — it meant designing a structure that could hold all five use cases without feeling like a compromise to users of any one of them.

Brand and visual inconsistency

The five experiences had evolved independently, with different visual maturity and patterns. Unifying them meant deciding what to standardize, what to preserve for continuity, and how to avoid eroding any one app's existing brand equity in the process.
